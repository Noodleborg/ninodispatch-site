What a missed call costs a plumber

A plain look at the math. Written for Houston-area plumbers, HVAC shops, and other local service businesses that live on the phone.

You're under a sink. The phone rings. You can't pick up. The caller waits a few rings, hangs up, and dials the next shop on Google. That one call may have been a water heater, a slab leak, or a weekend emergency. Here's a simple way to think about what it cost you.

A rough number

Industry groups and answering services often estimate that a typical residential plumbing or HVAC job lands somewhere around $250 to $500 once parts and labor are in. High-ticket jobs (water heaters, AC replacements) go much higher. You don't need exact averages to see the point:

  • Miss two solid jobs a month at $300 each and you've left about $600 on the table.
  • Miss one emergency water heater at $1,200 and that single call paid for months of help answering the phone.

These are example numbers, not a promise of what your shop will earn. Your average ticket is whatever it is. The idea is the same: a few missed calls add up fast.

Why people hang up

Most callers with a problem want a person now. They won't leave a voicemail. Studies from phone and answering services commonly report that a large share of callers (often more than half) hang up rather than leave a message when nobody answers. After hours and on weekends, that share goes up. You're busy when a lot of the best calls come in.

What "answering" actually means

Answering doesn't mean you have to drop your tools. It means someone (or something) picks up, takes the caller's name, number, address, and what they need, then gets that to you so you can call back or book them. Voicemail rarely does that. A receptionist who never sleeps does.
